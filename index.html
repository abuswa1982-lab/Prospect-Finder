How to use the Prospect Finder
  1. Customer Category is what kind of business you're looking for, written like you'd search Google, e.g. "commercial plumbing contractor" or "industrial safety equipment distributor."
  2. Location(s) takes one ZIP code or city per line. Add several lines to cover multiple areas in a single run, results across all of them get combined and deduplicated automatically.
  3. Radius controls how far out from each location to search. Google's practical limit is around 30 miles per point, entering a bigger area just means adding more location lines instead.
  4. Lead scoring gives every result a point total based on the rules you set (having a website, having a phone number, matching bonus keywords in the business name). Higher-scoring, more complete leads sort to the top.
  5. Save This Search Setup remembers your category, locations, and settings for next time, it does not save the results themselves, just the setup so you can rerun it fresh.
  6. When results come in, use Download Excel for a spreadsheet file, or Copy for Sheets to paste directly into an open Google Sheet.
"Analyze Seed" is one lookup. Then each location line is one billed request, same as the Prospect Finder. The seed business itself is dropped from the results automatically. Same geography rules apply: Google caps each point near 30 miles, so a whole state means adding more ZIP/city lines rather than one giant radius.
How to use Find Similar
  1. Seed customer is one company you already like. Add its city/state or ZIP so Google finds the exact one, and the website if you have it.
  2. Analyze Seed looks that business up and proposes a Customer Category. Review it and tweak it before searching — you stay in control of the wording.
  3. Location(s) works exactly like the Prospect Finder: one ZIP or city per line, tiled to cover as much area as you want. Not the whole country in one shot.
  4. Run it and the results drop into the same table, with the seed company itself left out. Download Excel or Copy for Sheets like usual.

Give it one center ZIP and a category. It fans out across the surrounding area in waves — searching a grid of points around your center — and keeps going until fresh results dry up or it hits your session cap. This is how you get past Google's 20-per-point ceiling: many points, merged and deduplicated into one list.

Each point searched is one billed request. A center plus one ring is 9 searches; plus a second ring is 25. The session cap stops the sweep before it runs away with your Google bill, and "stop when a wave adds fewer than N" halts it automatically once an area is picked clean. Google still returns up to 20 per point, so overlapping points are merged and deduplicated into one list.
How Area Sweep works
  1. Center ZIP is the middle of the area you want to cover. The sweep pins that spot, then searches outward from it.
  2. Waves are rings of search points around the center. Wave 0 is the center itself; wave 1 is a ring of 8 points around it; wave 2 is the next ring out, and so on. Each point is its own search returning up to 20 businesses, all merged and deduplicated.
  3. Per-point radius sets how big each point's circle is. Smaller circles with more points give tighter, more thorough coverage; bigger circles cover ground faster with fewer searches.
  4. Stop when a wave adds fewer than N is the auto-stop: once a fresh ring turns up almost no new businesses, the area's picked clean and the sweep ends on its own — exactly the "keep going until nothing new" behavior.
  5. Session cap is the hard ceiling on billed searches for one sweep, so it can never run away. Results build in the table below as each wave finishes; Download Excel or Copy for Sheets when it's done.

Looks for a column header containing "email" and, if present, one containing "name" or "company". Everything else in the sheet rides along untouched.

Rows sharing the same email domain are only researched once and the label is reused, so a batch of 100 customers from 30 companies is 30 lookups, not 100. Rows with no email, or with a personal email domain (gmail.com, yahoo.com, etc.), are skipped and marked rather than guessed at.
